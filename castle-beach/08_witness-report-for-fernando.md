## EXPERTS

### Steven Bernstein — DSM (Plaintiff) damages expert
Background: Forensic accounting. His report quantifies Valet Office $306,747 and Storage $360,729 (report total $667,476), the backbone of the damages case alongside your updated figures.
Why: proves the numbers. Expect a Daubert/foundation attack and a defense rebuttal expert on methodology.
Direct themes: data sources, the 50/50 valet basis, the storage-loss model, why the numbers are conservative.
Anticipated cross: reliability of inputs, assumptions about occupancy and rates, whether he double-counts.

### Alberti — DSM (Plaintiff) element/opinion expert
Background: Opinion that the valet room is a limited common element (LCE) rather than a general common element. This is the legal-physical linchpin of the valet claim (Item 2).
Why: if the valet room is an LCE tied to DSM's rights, the Association's "it is a common element, you had no right to the income" defense fails.
ACTION: confirm exact name, license, and scope from the 9/4/2025 Plaintiff Expert list PDF.
Direct themes: the governing documents, plats/surveys, the 1993 Amendment, why the room qualifies as LCE.

### Gregory Register — Castle Beach (Defendant) rebuttal expert
Background: Deposed. Opinion that the valet area is a common element (CE), NOT an LCE, relying on the 1993 Amendment 3 (deposition p. 95+). Directly opposes Alberti.
Why he matters: he is the Association's answer to our LCE theory. His deposition is already in hand, so we can pin him.
Cross-reference (his deposition): leans on the 1993 Amendment 3 for the CE characterization.
Proposed cross:
- Confirm he relies primarily on the 1993 Amendment 3; walk through its exact text.
- Whether he accounts for the 2007 Settlement Agreement and 1996 Hotel Agreement.
- Whether the physical configuration and exclusive use support LCE regardless of label.
- Any documents he did not review.
- His engagement, compensation, and prior testimony for condo associations.
