## PART III — COMMUNICATION / RESPONSE-TIME ANALYSIS

**Directly rebuts** Don Hayden's claim (June 9 & June 15, 2026): *"Any delays here are the result of your lack of communication"* and *"we went for weeks waiting for a response from you."*

**Method:** For each thread between MFH and the client side (Facundo Yebne / Mark Scott, the client's advisor), reply latency = time between one party's message and the other party's next message in that thread. Window covered: **Apr–Jun 2026** (the "recent months" Don put at issue). Earlier correspondence not included in this pass.

### Headline finding
- **Client side (Facundo/Mark): nearly every reply within hours** — typically **0.5–6 hours**, same day.
- **MFH/Don: repeated multi-day lags**, including a **4-day** reply in the fee thread, a **~2.8-day (67.5h)** reply, and the standout **11-day** stretch after the May 7 mediation in which Don never called the insurance adjuster about the **$300,000** offer.
- The **only** multi-day gap on the client's side was a **single, deliberate** ~7-day pause in the fee thread while deciding whether to authorize $30–40K/month more in fees and/or change counsel — a considered decision, not a failure to communicate.

### Bottom line for Part III
The record shows the **opposite** of Don's accusation: the client and his advisor answered within hours across the board, while the multi-day silences — and the consequential one (the $300K adjuster) — were on **MFH's** side. "We went for weeks waiting for a response" is not supported by the thread timestamps for this period; the one ~7-day client pause was a single, reasoned decision interval in the fee negotiation.
