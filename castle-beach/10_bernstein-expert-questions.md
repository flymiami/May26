Design Suites Miami v. Castle Beach, 2022-003970-CA-01. Our damages expert.
Eisner Advisory Group (EisnerAmper), Coral Gables. 45+ years, forensic accounting, economic damages, valuation, lost profits.
Disclosed scope: damages from (a) the Association's unauthorized use of the CU-14 valet stand, and (b) storage lost profits from the failure to maintain (exterior-wall/floor/ceiling flooding, burst pipes, toxic gas from the generator, white liquid from the ceiling).
Report figures on record: Valet Office $306,747 + Storage $360,729 = $667,476.

## A. Qualifications (direct)

## C. Valet damages (direct) — $306,747
- What the valet stand is and why it has rental/use value to CU-14.

## D. Storage lost profits (direct) — $360,729

- The decline: which units became unusable or unrentable and when.
- How you isolated losses caused by the Association's failure to maintain (flooding from exterior walls/floor/ceiling, burst pipes, generator gas, ceiling liquid, compactor contamination) from any other cause.

- The lost-profit calculation and the period.

## E. Update through 2026 (direct)
- Whether your figures are current through 2026 and, if updated, the new totals.
- How ongoing loss accrues (per month or per unit) so the number can be carried to trial and beyond.

## F. Cross-examination we must prepare him for
1. Valet is a money loser. Tsiolis testified the valet runs a $450,000 to $550,000 loss and 2021 revenue was about $188,091. Expect: "If the valet loses money, what did Design Suites lose?" His answer: the claim is the reasonable value of the appropriated limited common element and Design Suites' contractual share, which does not depend on the Association running the operation at a profit; the Association's self-selected expenses do not reduce the value of what it took.
2. Double counting. "Do the valet and storage numbers overlap?" Confirm they are separate spaces, separate income streams, no overlap.
3. Inputs and assumptions. "Whose numbers are these?" Be ready to show the source for occupancy, rates, and the baseline, and to distinguish actual records from estimates.
4. Causation for storage. "How do you know the flooding, not neglect by the owner or market conditions, caused the vacancy?" Point to the City violations, the Falcon admission, the dye test into Storage 4, and the tenant testimony (Draghi).
5. Mitigation. "Could the owner have rented these units or reduced the loss?" Be ready on why the units were not rentable in their condition.
6. Daubert / methodology. Expect a challenge to whether the method is generally accepted and reliably applied. Tie each step to accepted forensic and valuation standards.
7. Compensation and independence. His fee, who pays, and that the fee is not contingent on the outcome.
