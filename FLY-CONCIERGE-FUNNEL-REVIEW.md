# FLY Concierge Sales Funnel — Review & Proposed Changes

**Reviewed:** 2026-05-26
**Source:** [First Draft - Google Doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1Ou7Q3gN04SXTr7deL4PJa1C4-seeyFCyfRKvF26V6Wk/edit?usp=sharing)
**Context:** AI concierge / sales agent for FLY (Facundo Yebne) website, with Epic Hotel show next week.

---

## Overall Assessment

The funnel architecture is solid. The $17 front-end into $197/mo membership into $15K commission is a well-structured ascension ladder. The AI concierge concept is genuinely differentiated — it turns "browsing art" into a guided experience, which is the right move for buyers who don't see themselves as "art people."

**What works well:**
- The impulse-price entry ($17) with credit-toward-purchase mechanic removes all friction
- Story-driven selling (the duck, the artist, the mission) is the strongest differentiator vs. commodity art
- The Glow Map (Component 5) is a killer feature — UV/daylight dual personality is unique and gives buyers a reason to share
- The guarantee structure is clean and low-risk to the business
- The Flock membership is positioned as belonging, not consuming — correct for an artist brand

**What needs work:** pricing gaps, unclear tech readiness, missing urgency mechanics for the Epic Hotel show, and several [INFERRED] items that need resolution before this goes live.

## Section-by-Section Review

### 1. THE CORE OFFER ($17 FLY Concierge Pass)

#### Strengths
- "The buyer isn't browsing an art store. They're being walked to the one piece that belongs in their room by the artist himself" — this is the positioning line. Use it everywhere.
- Five-minute match promise is specific and testable.

#### Proposed Changes

**A. Clarify what "the AI" actually does at launch vs. later.**
The document describes a fully trained AI concierge that knows Facundo's entire catalog, matches room conditions, recommends sizes/finishes, and tells stories. For the Epic Hotel show *next week*, the question is: how much of this is built? If the AI isn't ready, the $17 pass should initially route to a human concierge flow (Facundo or a team member on WhatsApp/chat) with the same positioning. Call it "The FLY Concierge" either way — the buyer doesn't care whether the brain behind it is silicon or human. But the team needs to know what's shipping v1.

> **Action item:** Confirm what's live for Epic Hotel. If AI isn't ready, run human concierge at the show and use those conversations as training data for the AI build.

**B. Component 2 (Reserve) — real-time inventory is a hard technical requirement.**
The doc flags this as [INFERRED]. If inventory/hold functionality isn't wired up, Component 2 is a promise the system can't keep. For the show, a simpler version works: "Message the concierge to hold a piece" with manual confirmation. Don't promise real-time availability unless it's built.

> **Action item:** Confirm inventory system status. If not ready, simplify to manual holds via WhatsApp/DM for Epic Hotel launch.

**C. Component 3 (Custom Commission via Concierge) — missing price anchor.**
The component says commissions start at $2,500, but the high-ticket section says $15,000. The gap is huge and unexplained. A buyer reading "$2,500 commissions" here and then seeing $15,000 later will feel bait-and-switched.

> **Proposed fix:** Reframe Component 3 as "Commission Discovery" — the concierge captures the brief and routes to the studio, where pricing starts at $2,500 for prints/small custom pieces and scales to $15,000+ for signature installations. Make the range explicit so the buyer self-selects into the right tier.

**D. Component 5 (Glow Map) — this should be the HERO demo at the show.**
UV-reactive art that transforms at night is visual, shareable, and unforgettable. At the Epic Hotel show, this is the thing people will photograph and post. The funnel buries it as Component 5.

> **Proposed fix:** Move the Glow Map experience earlier in the concierge flow (after the match, before the reserve). At the show, make the UV reveal a live demo moment — lights go down, UV comes up, crowd reacts. Build the concierge flow to mirror this: "Here's your piece in daylight... now here it is at night." This is the viral moment.

### 2. ORDER BUMPS

#### Order Bump 1 — The Gift ($37)

**Issue:** The $37 is for the "gift-design service," not the gift itself. The cheapest actual piece (a signed mini duck or print) starts at $50 per the v3 plan. So the buyer pays $37 for the matching service and then still has to buy the gift piece separately. This feels like paying twice.

> **Proposed fix:** Either (a) bundle a mini duck or small print INTO the $37 bump so the buyer walks away with an actual gift, or (b) make the bump $47-$57 and include the physical piece. The emotional promise ("the gift they'll FaceTime their family about") demands a tangible deliverable, not a service receipt.

#### Order Bump 2 — Whole-Home Curation ($97)

**This is strong as-is** for designers and hospitality buyers. One suggestion: at the Epic Hotel show, rename this to something like "The Property Plan" for the hospitality audience in the room. Interior designers and hotel buyers think in properties and projects, not "homes."

> **Proposed fix:** Offer two framings — "Whole-Home Curation" for residential, "The Property Collection" for trade/hospitality. Same $97 service, different language for different buyers.

### 3. RESULTS GUARANTEE

The Love-It-On-Your-Wall Guarantee is clean. Two notes:

1. **The $17 credit mechanic is still [INFERRED].** This is the linchpin of the whole front-end. If the $17 isn't credited toward the first purchase, it's a $17 fee for an AI chat — much harder sell. Confirm and make this explicit in all copy.

2. **The 14-day exchange on physical pieces needs a clear policy page.** At $15K commission level, "14-day no-questions exchange" could be financially devastating. The high-ticket guarantee (Until-You-Love-It) is conditional and milestone-based, which is correct — but make sure the 14-day unconditional guarantee only applies to catalog pieces, NOT to commissions.

> **Action item:** Confirm $17 credit mechanic. Separate guarantee language for catalog pieces (14-day unconditional) vs. commissions (milestone-based conditional).

### 4. ASCENSION PATH

The ladder logic is sound. Two gaps:

**A. The jump from $97 (order bump) to $197/month (membership) has no stepping stone.**
A buyer who just spent $17 + $97 = $114 is being asked to commit to $197/month ($2,364/year). That's a 20x jump in annual spend with no intermediate offer. Consider a "first month free" or "founding member rate" ($97/mo for the first 50 members) to bridge the gap, especially at launch.

> **Proposed fix:** Add a founding-member rate for The Flock — $97/month for the first 50 members who join during or within 30 days of the Epic Hotel show. This creates urgency, rewards early adopters, and makes the jump from the core offer feel proportional.

**B. The jump from $197/month to $15,000 is intentionally large — but the trigger is vague.**
"After time in The Flock" doesn't define when or how the high-ticket offer gets surfaced. The concierge AI should have a specific trigger: e.g., after the member's 3rd month, or after they've purchased 2+ pieces, or after they ask about a commission. Define the trigger so it can be automated.

> **Proposed fix:** Define 2-3 explicit triggers for surfacing the $15K offer: (1) member asks about custom/commission work, (2) member has been active 90+ days, (3) member has purchased 2+ pieces. Let the concierge AI handle the introduction naturally.

### 5. THE FLOCK ($197/month Membership)

This is the strongest section in the document. The positioning as "belong to the movement" rather than "buy a subscription" is exactly right for an artist brand.

#### Proposed Changes

**A. The "monthly live group call with Facundo" will be the first thing to break.**
Facundo is already stretched thin (the doc acknowledges this). Monthly is sustainable at 50 members. At 500 members, a single monthly call becomes a webinar, not a community touchpoint. Plan for this now.

> **Proposed fix:** Start monthly. At 100+ members, shift to quarterly live calls with Facundo + monthly "Studio Dispatch" (a 5-10 minute recorded video from the studio showing what he's working on). This scales without losing intimacy and is actually more authentic — artists work in studios, not on Zoom.

**B. "The Story Exchange" needs a platform decision.**
A members-only community space is mentioned but not specified. Discord? A custom platform? A WhatsApp group? The choice matters for the audience. High-income homeowners and interior designers are NOT Discord people.

> **Proposed fix:** For launch, use a private WhatsApp Community or a simple Circle.so / Mighty Networks space. The concierge AI can post prompts and moderate. Avoid Discord — it signals gaming/crypto, not art collecting.

**C. Trade-up credit is a great mechanic but needs terms.**
"Apply what they've spent toward a bigger piece" — does this mean membership fees count as credit? Or only previous piece purchases? This is a significant financial commitment either way and needs clear terms before launch.

> **Action item:** Define trade-up credit terms. Recommendation: credit previous piece purchases at 50-80% of original price toward a larger piece. Membership fees should NOT count as trade-up credit (that makes the membership feel transactional, not communal).

### 6. THE FLY SIGNATURE COMMISSION ($15,000)

Well-structured. The conditional guarantee (milestone-based, requires buyer participation) is smart — it protects Facundo from ghost clients. Two notes:

**A. "2 to 3 commissions per month" capacity is aggressive.**
If each commission takes 60-90 days with bi-weekly design sessions, 2-3 new commissions per month means Facundo is running 4-9 concurrent commissions at steady state, each with bi-weekly 1-on-1 calls. That's 8-18 client calls per month on top of the Flock group call, shows, and actually making art. This will burn out fast.

> **Proposed fix:** Cap at 1-2 new commissions per month, with a public waitlist that reinforces exclusivity. "Facundo currently has 2 commission slots open for Q3" is better positioning than "commissions available" anyway.

**B. Missing: what happens after the commission is delivered?**
The relationship doesn't end at installation. These are your highest-value clients. The document doesn't describe post-commission nurture — are they automatically in The Flock? Do they get priority for future shows? A collector who spent $15K should feel permanently inside the inner circle.

> **Proposed fix:** All Signature Commission clients get lifetime Flock membership (included, not billed) and "Collector Circle" status — first call for new originals, private studio visits, and priority invitations to every show. This turns a $15K one-time into a lifetime relationship and referral engine.

## EPIC HOTEL SHOW — SPECIFIC RECOMMENDATIONS

Since the show is next week, here's what needs to be ready vs. what can wait:

### Must Have for the Show
1. **The $17 Concierge Pass** — even if the AI isn't fully built, run it as a human-assisted concierge via WhatsApp/chat. The pass is the lead capture mechanism.
2. **The Glow Map demo** — UV reveal as a live moment at the show. This IS the show.
3. **A clear one-pager or card** with the offer, the guarantee, and how to buy. Physical takeaway.
4. **Payment processing** for the $17 pass, ideally with the order bumps available at checkout.

### Can Wait Until After the Show
1. Full AI concierge build (use show conversations as training data)
2. Real-time inventory/hold system
3. The Flock membership platform
4. Automated ascension triggers

### Show-Specific Addition: The Epic Hotel Founding Collector Offer
Create urgency with a show-exclusive: "The first 10 people who join The Flock at the Epic Hotel show get founding-member pricing ($97/month instead of $197) for life, plus a signed show-exclusive mini duck." This gives the sales agent a specific, time-limited offer to close with at the show.
