Ninety percent of the revenue is the store. The Clover terminal settles almost every day — 212 batches so far this year, averaging $1,681 — and that is $353,613 of the $393,470. The rest is the rent roll on CU-14: $39,234 of billed rent and $623 of ATM commission.

So there are really only two businesses to manage, and they are in very different states. The store works and is roughly flat. The rent roll is billed but not collected.

The store: flat, not growing

89.9% of revenue

Comparing like for like, June–July 2025 did $91,402 and June–July 2026 did $95,384 — up 4.4%. August is tracking at $1,315/day, which annualizes to about $40,800 against $42,606 last August. Call it flat.

Flat is the honest read: the store throws off real cash, but nothing in the last fifteen months has moved it to a new level. The upside here is operational — basket size, mix, hours, and the card processing rate — not a new line of business.

Costing you now

Merchant fees ran $19,729 on $356,325 of card volume — an effective 5.54%. Card-present retail typically blends between 2.6% and 3.2%. If that gap is real and not a mix artifact, you are paying roughly $8,200/year more than you need to, and it comes straight off the bottom line. Pull three Clover statements and put them out for a rate review.

The rent roll is billed, not collected

$53,508 outstanding

This is the finding that matters most, and it has a single mechanical cause. You have four active monthly recurring invoice templates in QuickBooks. All four generate an invoice on the 1st of every month. All four have auto-pay set to OFF. The system has been faithfully producing paper and collecting nothing.

The 2Nice line is not a receivable. It is a −$15,074 year-end closing journal entry (Asiento de cierre, 31 Dec 2024) parked in accounts receivable, where it quietly nets against real balances. Your true gross receivable is $68,582, not $53,508. Move that entry out of A/R.

And Girasole may already have paid part of theirs. None of that appears applied against the open invoices. Reconcile it before you chase them, or you will demand money you have already received.

Deadline you may not have noticed

The Miami Cheap Tours recurring template expires 1 September 2026. They owe $38,500, have not paid since roughly October 2025, and their billing arrangement lapses in two weeks. That date is leverage, and it is the one hard deadline on this page.

The profit number is not real

~$76,000 overstated

QuickBooks shows $132,980 of net income on a 64% gross margin. A convenience store does not run a 64% gross margin. What actually happened is that inventory purchases stopped being recorded partway through the year.

Apply the Jan–April margin of 44.7% — the only stretch where the books look complete — to the full year to date, and gross profit is about $175,700, not $251,834. That is a $76,100 overstatement, and it means the reported profit is off by roughly the same amount.

Restated operating income is therefore around $56,900. Then two large costs are missing from the P&L entirely:

  • Labor. There is no wages line anywhere in the P&L, yet you paid $1,283 in payroll processing fees and carry wages payable and payroll tax liabilities on the balance sheet. A store open daily has staff. That cost is sitting in another entity — DSM Payroll Corp appears in your intercompany accounts — and needs to be pulled into this view.
  • Interest. You carry $1,293,714 of long-term debt — a $660,000 note, a $449,796 SBA EIDL, $170,797 to Travel Rentals — and the P&L books zero interest expense. The EIDL alone at 3.75% is roughly $16,900 a year.

Add realistic figures for those two and the year is somewhere between breakeven and a loss. I am not going to invent the numbers — but you should put them in, because every decision below depends on knowing which side of zero you are on.

Two more balance-sheet items to resolve

Undeposited Funds sits at $114,891 against a negative bank balance. Either that is real money that never cleared into an account, or it is a posting error. Both are worth finding out about this week.

Florida Department of Revenue payable is $24,776, and the City of Miami Beach resort tax audit (RTX 2145511, covering 2022–2024) is still open with the auditor stating that documentation supporting your claimed exemptions has not been received. That is an unquantified liability sitting on top of everything else.
