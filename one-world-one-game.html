The Show

A love letter to everything that unites us

ONE WORLD, ONE GAME is staged in a summer when the world turns its eyes to the same field. The exhibition braids together three celebrations of belonging: the FIFA World Cup, Pride, and the 250th anniversary of the United States.

Through FLY's signature ducks — sculpted from thousands of tiny resin figures and finished in jersey stripes, flags, and the colors of nations — he reimagines the beautiful game as a metaphor for the human one. Every team, every flag, every fan stands shoulder to shoulder. UnityBeak returns as the show's beating heart, joined by new works including a World Cup Duck crowned with a soccer-ball head and the flags of the competing nations, and a Brazilian jersey piece created for the occasion.

At its core, the show asks a simple question: if we can come together for a game, why not for each other? Playful, monumental, and unmistakably Miami, ONE WORLD, ONE GAME invites every visitor, from every corner of the world, onto the same team.

The Artist

Facundo Yebne — FLY

Facundo Yebne, known as FLY, is a Miami-based artist whose joyful, large-scale work turns the humble rubber duck into a universal symbol of connection, unconditional love, and community. An engineer and entrepreneur by training, FLY discovered his artistic calling in early 2024.

What began as experimentation — with neon, LED, optic fiber, and thousands of tiny resin ducks — quickly grew into ambitious public art. He debuted at Red Dot Miami (December 2024), collaborated with BOSS at Merrick Park, and in March 2025 created UnityBeak, a pair of monumental Rubber Ducks commissioned by the Lincoln Road BID and the City of Miami Beach for International Pride Month. The work moved nearly everyone who passed it and caught the eye of the Florence Biennale, which invited him to its XV edition in October 2025 — where he created UnityBeak Firenze hand-in-hand with the local community and won an award for Installation Art.

FLY serves on the City of Miami Beach LGBTQIA+ Advisory Committee. He believes art belongs where everyone can reach it — sparking conversation, memory, and joy.
