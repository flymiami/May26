ONE WORLD. ONE GAME.

Award-winning Miami artist FLY turns thousands of rubber ducks into a World Cup, a Pride celebration, and a love letter to two nations. Kimpton EPIC Hotel · June 3 to July 27, 2026.
As featured in  Time Out  ·  GayCities  ·  Wire Magazine  ·  Artburst Miami  ·  Revista Viagem
Soccer is the one language the whole planet speaks. I build my art the same way, one small duck at a time, until thousands of them become something we all recognize.Facundo Yebne, FLY
One World, One Game · the 36 by 36 inch namesake canvas

MIAMI, FLORIDA, USA — June 3, 2026. As the world arrives for the 2026 FIFA World Cup, Facundo Yebne, the American-Argentine artist known as FLY, opens ONE WORLD. ONE GAME. at the Kimpton EPIC Hotel in downtown Miami. On view June 3 to July 27, 2026, the exhibition fills the hotel lobby and 16th floor with large scale pop art built entirely from thousands of mini rubber and resin ducks.

The show brings together three summer moments: the FIFA World Cup, Miami Pride, and the 250th anniversary of the United States. Its message is unity. Thousands of identical little ducks, placed by hand, become images the whole world recognizes.

The Exhibition

In the lobby stands UnityBeak Trilogy: Love, a single sculpture of thousands of mini rubber ducks, the first thing every guest meets.

The 16th floor opens onto a right wall built like a World Cup fixture. Groups sit on each side and advance toward the center. As nations move forward, their jerseys are built in ducks and hung, so the wall changes through the tournament, from the USA at one end to Argentina at the other.

On the USA side, FLY's adopted home and the tournament host: Don't Kuack With Liberty, America Peace, United Kuakies of America, and the Pulisic jersey. On the Argentina side, his birthplace and the defending champion: a flag with a glow in the dark center, a heart in yellow, white and light blue, and the Messi jersey. Between them hang the flags of Mexico, Japan, Brazil and France, the section that updates as teams advance.

At the center hangs the show's namesake. One World, One Game is a 36 by 36 inch canvas, a circle split in four: the American flag, a glow in the dark soccer ball, the colors of the rainbow, and the South American continent.

Glass doors open to the terrace, where Proud Love Pink stops passers by. The left wall holds a giant Polaroid selfie spot, edged in rubber ducks for guests to sign, beside a peace sign and a smiley in the colors of the Pride flag. An interactive kiosk tells the story of every work in the artist's own voice. Return during the tournament and the show is different each week.

Concept

FLY's work sits in the tradition of Pop and Conceptual Art, recalling Claes Oldenburg's transformation of the ordinary into the monumental and Ai Weiwei's reinterpretation of common objects as social commentary. The rubber duck, familiar and universal, becomes a metaphor for resilience, community and unity. Each work is built from thousands of mini resin ducks of many sizes, placed by hand on conservation grade panels, and signed with a Certificate of Authenticity.

About the Artist

Facundo Yebne (FLY) is an American-Argentine artist whose practice began in 2024, after twenty-five years of entrepreneurial success in real estate, hospitality and wellness between Miami and Buenos Aires. He debuted at Red Dot Miami during Art Basel, handing out rubber ducks and inviting visitors to sign inflatable ducks. The City of Miami Beach then commissioned two public Pride installations on Lincoln Road, shown April 2 to July 1, 2025: UnityBeak, two giant ducks built from 14,000 mini rubber ducks, and Proud Love, a 10 foot heart built from 7,000 rubber ducks, which together drew thousands of visitors and collectors. In 2025 he won Fourth Prize for Installation Art at the XV Florence Biennale. ONE WORLD. ONE GAME. holds 13 one of a kind originals, from $1,000 to $10,000, available at flymiami.art.
