ONE WORLD. ONE GAME.

A FLY Miami exhibition by Facundo Yebne at the Kimpton EPIC Hotel, Downtown Miami. Thousands of rubber and resin ducks, built around the 2026 FIFA World Cup, Miami Pride, and the 250th anniversary of the United States.

MIAMI, FL. Facundo Yebne, known as FLY, presents ONE WORLD. ONE GAME. at the Kimpton EPIC Hotel. The show lives in two spaces, the lobby and the 16th floor, and is built entirely from thousands of mini rubber and resin ducks.

Three threads run through it: the 2026 FIFA World Cup, Miami Pride, and the 250th anniversary of the United States. The message is unity. Identical little ducks, placed by hand, become images the whole world recognizes.

In the lobby stands UnityBeak Trilogy: Love, a single sculpture of thousands of mini rubber ducks. It is the only piece downstairs, and the first thing every guest meets.

The 16th floor opens onto a right wall built like a World Cup fixture. Groups sit on each side and move toward the center. As nations advance, their jerseys are built in ducks and hung, so the wall keeps changing through the tournament. It runs from the USA at one end to Argentina at the other.

USA side, FLY's adopted home and the tournament host: Don't Kuack With Liberty, the Statue of Liberty in solid tiny resin ducks; America Peace in mini rubber ducks; United Kuakies of America in solid tiny resin ducks; and the Pulisic jersey for the USA.

Argentina side, FLY's birthplace and the defending champion: a flag in mini rubber ducks with a glow in the dark resin center; a heart in yellow, white and light blue for the Argentine flag; and the Messi jersey. Between the ends hang the flags of Mexico, Japan, Brazil and France, the rotating section that updates as teams advance.

At the center is the show's namesake. One World, One Game is a 36 by 36 inch canvas with a circle split in four: the American flag in mini rubber ducks, a soccer ball in glow in the dark resin ducks, the colors of the rainbow, and the South American continent.

Glass doors at the end of the wall open to the terrace, where Proud Love Pink stops passers by. The left wall holds a giant Polaroid selfie spot, edged in rubber ducks of many colors and sizes with a white center for guests to sign, alongside a peace sign and a smiley face in the colors of the Pride flag.

An interactive kiosk tells the story of each work in the artist's own voice. The show holds 13 one of a kind originals, from $1,000 to $10,000, each signed with a Certificate of Authenticity and viewable at flymiami.art.

One material, one message: peace, love, and joy. I place thousands of rubber and resin ducks by hand until they become something we all recognize.Facundo Yebne, FLY
